History & Culture

The midway appetite

Long before dessert menus chased novelty for its own sake, the midway appetite was doing the trick honestly — one scoop, one costume, one grin at a time.

Midway eating is walking eating — food as souvenir, spectacle, and supper at once. The ice cream potato was built for exactly this appetite: it photographs, it travels, it tells a story by the second bite. Desserts that need a table need not apply; the dish needs only a spoon and an audience.

One scoop, one dusting, one split — the classic grammar is short enough to memorize, and the midway appetite is written in it, however fancy the dialect gets.

Ask a booth veteran what matters and they will not say talent — they will say cold, clean, and quick, the three virtues the midway appetite is judged by.

If you are meeting this dish for the first time through the midway appetite, welcome to one of dessert's longest-running gags — and one of its most affectionate ones.

However it is dressed, the midway appetite keeps the promise the dish has always made: first it fools you, then it feeds you, and it never once apologizes for the joke.

A glass dish, a long spoon, a paper napkin: the liturgy around the midway appetite is as plain as the dish itself, and that plainness is the elegance. It is why the midway appetite earns its place in the cabinet.

The dish's home turf is potato country, and the connection is the whole point of the midway appetite: a dessert that honors the russet by impersonating it, served in the places where the real thing built the local pride. Readers tend to remember the midway appetite long after the fair closes.

The midway sold thrills by the minute, but the midway appetite sold something slower: a small ceremony of disbelief, performed at a counter, one eater at a time. It is one more reason the midway appetite rewards the curious.

Presentation is half the recipe this reference will never print — a chilled plate, a confident split, toppings placed like you mean it — and nowhere is that plainer than in the midway appetite: the other half is simply not rushing the freeze. That is the company the midway appetite keeps, and it is good company.
