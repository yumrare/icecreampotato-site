The chocolate-dipped skin: the build

The first time most people meet the chocolate-dipped skin, somebody older is smiling — the dish travels from hand to hand like a family joke that never wears out.

As a build, the chocolate-dipped skin — the molded scoop dipped in a thin chocolate shell instead of dusted — a crisper, glossier jacket. True to its name, the chocolate-dipped skin departs from the classic at precisely the point it advertises — and nowhere else.

The plain build is the control in every experiment this reference runs, and when the chocolate-dipped skin departs from it, the departure is measured against that plain, cocoa-dusted standard.

Speed is a flavor in fair food, and the chocolate-dipped skin, served before the costume softens, tastes like the fair itself: urgent, sweet, and gone too soon.

You do not need to have grown up at the fair to follow the chocolate-dipped skin — but by the end, you may feel as if you had.

Another entry filed, another serving remembered: the chocolate-dipped skin joins the cabinet the way every great fair story does — completely, and with affection.

The straight face is the dish's oldest serving tool, and the chocolate-dipped skin is served with it still: no wink, no apology, just a potato that is not one, set down like dinner. And the regulars would not have the chocolate-dipped skin any other way.

The dish's home turf is potato country, and the connection is the whole point of the chocolate-dipped skin: a dessert that honors the russet by impersonating it, served in the places where the real thing built the local pride. Fooling the eye, feeding the spoon: the chocolate-dipped skin goes on doing both.

The midway sold thrills by the minute, but the chocolate-dipped skin sold something slower: a small ceremony of disbelief, performed at a counter, one eater at a time. That is how the chocolate-dipped skin has always closed the show.

It helps to remember what era invented this dish's fame — the age of the soda fountain and the county fair, when a dessert was expected to be a little bit of a show — because the chocolate-dipped skin is that era, still serving. The cabinet takes the chocolate-dipped skin in the way it takes all its own: like it was always there.
