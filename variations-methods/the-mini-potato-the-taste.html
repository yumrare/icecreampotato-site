The mini potato: the taste

Long before dessert menus chased novelty for its own sake, the mini potato was doing the trick honestly — one scoop, one costume, one grin at a time.

On the tongue, the mini potato — a half-size scoop molded small, built for parties and samplers; the costume survives the shrinking — changes the classic's balance in a direction its fans defend warmly: the cocoa-and-vanilla chord gains a new note without losing the tune.

One scoop, one dusting, one split — the classic grammar is short enough to memorize, and the mini potato is written in it, however fancy the dialect gets.

Ask a booth veteran what matters and they will not say talent — they will say cold, clean, and quick, the three virtues the mini potato is judged by.

Bring a spoon and a little patience for wonder, because the mini potato repays both — first with the laugh, then with the eating.

In the end, the mini potato is the dish in miniature: vanilla, cocoa, cream, and nerve, arranged to look like dinner and taste like childhood.

Diner counters kept this dish alive between fairs, and the mini potato carries that counter culture in it — the long spoon, the glass dish, the regular who orders it to watch a newcomer's face. That is how the mini potato has always closed the show.

The soda fountain is mostly gone and the county fair is mostly changed, but the mini potato persists in both places' memory — and on enough menus to keep the memory fed. The fair is in the mini potato, every time.

Generations of eaters have played along with the mini potato at first sight, and a costume dessert asks for nothing more: the happy pretense that dinner came early. It is one more reason the mini potato rewards the curious.

It is easy to forget, watching the costume work, that the mini potato is also a lesson in restraint — one scoop, one skin, one split, nothing wasted. That is the pleasure of the mini potato, plainly stated.

There is a reason the mini potato belongs to summer: the joke needs an audience, the costume needs the cold, and both need the long light of a fair evening to land. It is the sort of thing the mini potato has always done best.
