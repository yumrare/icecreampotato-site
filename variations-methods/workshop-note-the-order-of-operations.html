Workshop note: the order of operations

What the midway knew, and the soda fountain agreed with, is that appetite and laughter are neighbors — and the order of operations has lived on that border its whole life.

Freeze, dust, return, split, sauce, nuts, dollop, serve. The order is the method; every troubleshooting entry in this reference is a story about the order being broken. Write it on the booth wall. The old houses did.

At the center of the cabinet stands the plain skin-on scoop, patient as a museum piece, while the order of operations and its cousins circle it like exhibits around a founder.

A novelty asks to be looked at; a classic asks to be eaten; and the order of operations, at its best, manages to be asked both questions at once.

Some entries you read for facts and some for the pleasure of the thing, and the order of operations is shelved, deliberately, where those two pleasures meet.

Some desserts are remembered by their recipes; this one is remembered by its readings, and the order of operations is now one of them.

A costume dessert succeeds when the eater plays along, and players have never been scarce for the order of operations: generations have happily pretended, at first sight, that dinner had arrived early. And the regulars would not have the order of operations any other way.

Since the name causes grins at every counter, the record should be straight about the order of operations: no potato, not now, not ever — just ice cream wearing russet drag with a cocoa skin. The cabinet keeps the order of operations for exactly this reason.

The midway sold thrills by the minute, but the order of operations sold something slower: a small ceremony of disbelief, performed at a counter, one eater at a time. It is the sort of thing the order of operations has always done best.

It helps to remember what era invented this dish's fame — the age of the soda fountain and the county fair, when a dessert was expected to be a little bit of a show — because the order of operations is that era, still serving. And that is the quiet charm of the order of operations.

Method note — the order of operations is described here as a way of making, not a recipe; the reference documents methods and lore, and publishes no exact recipes.
