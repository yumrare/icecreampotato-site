The cone presentation: where it belongs

Some entries in this cabinet are curiosities and some are classics, and the cone presentation manages to be both at once, which is precisely why it earns its page.

Every variation has a home ground, and the cone presentation — the molded scoop perched on a cone, skin and all; the midway walking version — belongs to the counters and occasions that invented it: it reads best where it was born, and travels as a story about that place.

Strip the dish to its blueprint and you get an oval of firm vanilla under a dark dusting, opened once — the spare drawing that the cone presentation decorates in its own way.

Cocoa is both paint and seasoning here, and the hand that dusts the cone presentation is doing two jobs with one gesture, whether it knows it or not.

The double-take is the dish's handshake, and the cone presentation offers it to every reader who walks up to the counter.

And with that, the cone presentation is filed where the curious can find it — in the cabinet, under the fair lights, next to the classics.

The straight face is the dish's oldest serving tool, and the cone presentation is served with it still: no wink, no apology, just a potato that is not one, set down like dinner. Readers tend to remember the cone presentation long after the fair closes.

There is no potato in the cone presentation — there never was — and that cheerful fraud, ice cream in russet drag under a cocoa skin, is the whole point of the name that grins back from every menu. And the cone presentation wears it well.

A glass dish, a long spoon, a paper napkin: the liturgy around the cone presentation is as plain as the dish itself, and that plainness is the elegance. And the regulars would not have the cone presentation any other way.

The dish's home turf is potato country, and the connection is the whole point of the cone presentation: a dessert that honors the russet by impersonating it, served in the places where the real thing built the local pride. The story is in the cone presentation, and it needs no words to be told.

This reference — Ice Cream Potato — documents the dish and nothing else, and the cone presentation is part of that whole: build, variations, methods, lore.
