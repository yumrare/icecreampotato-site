The Dish

The cocoa skin: the classic way

A dessert can be famous for flavor or famous for nerve, and the cocoa skin has always traded on the second while quietly delivering the first.

The classic handling of the cocoa skin is restraint. A dusting of cocoa powder over the frozen scoop, mottled like a real russet jacket. Onto a hard-frozen surface, applied dry: that is how it remains a skin and not a sauce. Thousands of servings taught the booths how the cocoa skin is handled, and the lesson they kept was the plain one: the same way, every time.

A baked potato that ran away to the dessert case — that is the founding image, and the cocoa skin keeps faith with it every time the costume goes on.

There is no hiding a shortcut in a dish this plain, which is why the cocoa skin exposes lazy freezing the moment the spoon goes in.

For the curious reader, the joy of the cocoa skin is the double-take — the half second where the eye insists it is looking at a baked potato and the spoon insists otherwise.

File the cocoa skin where it belongs — in the cabinet of curiosities this reference keeps, between the classics and the joyful oddballs, all of them ice cream potatoes at heart.

There is showmanship in the cocoa skin, but it is the honest kind — the kind that hides nothing, since the whole trick is announced by the name on the menu. The curious could do worse than start with the cocoa skin.

It helps to remember what era invented this dish's fame — the age of the soda fountain and the county fair, when a dessert was expected to be a little bit of a show — because the cocoa skin is that era, still serving. It is why the cocoa skin earns its place in the cabinet.

The fair's golden hour — lights on, air cooling — is when the cocoa skin is most itself: cold, sweet, and faintly ridiculous in the best light America makes. And so, thanks to the cocoa skin, the eye goes on being fooled and the spoon goes on being fed.

Diner counters kept this dish alive between fairs, and the cocoa skin carries that counter culture in it — the long spoon, the glass dish, the regular who orders it to watch a newcomer's face. The fair is in the cocoa skin, every time.
