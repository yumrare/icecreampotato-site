The chocolate-sauce gravy: what it is

The old fairs sold wonder by the ticket, and tucked into that economy of wonder, the chocolate-sauce gravy sold the cheapest astonishment on the grounds: a potato that was not a potato.

Definition first: the chocolate-sauce gravy is A ribbon of chocolate sauce poured like gravy over the split. It darkens the reading of the dish from baked potato toward sundae — which is precisely its charm as a variation. the chocolate-sauce gravy sits in the dish's anatomy as structure, not decoration: one of the few parts the classic build cannot do without.

The classic build asks little and promises much: ice cream that holds a shape, cocoa that reads as skin, a split that sells the story — and the chocolate-sauce gravy inherits both the ask and the promise.

The old booths judged a build at arm's length first — asking of the chocolate-sauce gravy the only question that matters at a glance: does it read as a potato? — and the chocolate-sauce gravy passes or fails that squint test before flavor ever gets a vote.

Bring a spoon and a little patience for wonder, because the chocolate-sauce gravy repays both — first with the laugh, then with the eating.

In the end, the chocolate-sauce gravy is the dish in miniature: vanilla, cocoa, cream, and nerve, arranged to look like dinner and taste like childhood.

It causes grins at every counter, the name of the chocolate-sauce gravy — and no wonder: there is no potato in it, only ice cream in russet drag, cocoa for skin, a split for a smile. the chocolate-sauce gravy tells it as a story — no words required.

There is showmanship in the chocolate-sauce gravy, but it is the honest kind — the kind that hides nothing, since the whole trick is announced by the name on the menu. Readers tend to remember the chocolate-sauce gravy long after the fair closes.

Somewhere behind the chocolate-sauce gravy stands a booth hand with a straight face and a fast scoop, and the whole tradition travels in that posture: serve it plain, serve it proud, never explain the joke. And the regulars would not have the chocolate-sauce gravy any other way.
