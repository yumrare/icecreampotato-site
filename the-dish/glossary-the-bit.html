The Dish

Glossary: the bit

If the fairground had a pantry of legends, the bit would sit on the shelf between the prize pig and the giant pumpkin — homegrown, unlikely, and entirely beloved.

In the language of the dish, the bit means this: The impersonation itself — the whole committed performance of potato-ness that the dish asks of its makers. The reference keeps a glossary because the dish grew its own vocabulary at fair booths and diner counters — half kitchen shorthand, half affectionate slang — and a curious reader deserves the translation — the bit very much included.

The costume only works because the classic underneath is disciplined: a scoop frozen hard enough to hold its oval, and the bit leans on that discipline in every bite.

Look closely at the bit and the workmanship shows — in the mottling of the skin, the shoulders of the split, the peak of the cream — the way stitching shows on a good glove.

There is no wrong door into this cabinet, and the bit is as good a door as any — it opens onto the whole curious collection.

When the history of fair food is told with the affection it deserves, the bit will be in it — not as a footnote, but as a flavor.

The midway sold thrills by the minute, but the bit sold something slower: a small ceremony of disbelief, performed at a counter, one eater at a time. That, in the end, is what the bit is for.

The dish's home turf is potato country, and the connection is the whole point of the bit: a dessert that honors the russet by impersonating it, served in the places where the real thing built the local pride. The cabinet takes the bit in the way it takes all its own: like it was always there.

Some novelties age into embarrassments; the bit aged into a tradition, because underneath the costume there was always a genuinely good dessert keeping the joke honest. It is why the bit earns its place in the cabinet.

Old-timers at the fair will tell you the bit outsells its own punchline — people come for the gag and come back for the eating, summer after summer. And the bit goes on — eye fooled, spoon fed, summer after summer.

The cabinet closes on the bit the way it closes on every entry: the dish documented whole, in the Ice Cream Potato reference.
